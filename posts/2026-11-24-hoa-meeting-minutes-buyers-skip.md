# HOA Meeting Minutes: The Document Most Buyers Skip Before Signing

Before you buy into a homeowners association, you'll read the fees, the rules and the amenities list. Almost nobody reads the meeting minutes — and the minutes are the one document that tells you what living there is actually like.

## What meeting minutes are

Every HOA board keeps minutes: a written record of what was discussed and decided at each board meeting. They're not marketing. They record the arguments, the votes, the complaints and the problems the board is trying to solve. That makes them the most honest document an association produces.

## What to look for in them

**Money problems, in plain language.** Minutes record special assessments being discussed, reserve funds running low, or a big repair the board is putting off. If the last year of minutes mentions a roof fund shortfall three times, the fees you're quoted today are not the fees you'll pay next year.

**Recurring complaints.** The same issue appearing meeting after meeting — parking disputes, noise, a broken gate, a neighbour's unapproved renovation — tells you what daily life in the community actually involves, and how slowly the board resolves things.

**How the board treats owners.** Minutes show whether the board listens or lectures, whether decisions are explained or simply announced, and whether owners who raise concerns get answers. You're not just buying a home; you're joining a small local government.

## How to get them

Ask your agent or the seller for the last 12 months of minutes before you make an offer. In most states, buyers are entitled to review association documents during the due-diligence period. If the seller or the association won't share them, treat that as information too — a well-run association has nothing to hide in its minutes.

## Read them with the budget in mind

Minutes and fees belong together. A community with low fees and minutes full of deferred repairs is not a bargain — it's a special assessment waiting to happen. A community with higher fees and minutes that show a funded reserve and finished projects is charging you for maintenance you're going to need anyway.
