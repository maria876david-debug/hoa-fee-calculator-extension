# Can You Rent Out Your Home in an HOA? The Rules Most Owners Miss

Buying a home in an HOA community and planning to rent it out later sounds simple — it is your home, after all. But in many HOA communities, renting is one of the most tightly controlled things an owner can do, and the rules are written in the documents most buyers never read. Finding out after you have bought is an expensive lesson.

## The rental cap is the rule that surprises people most

Many HOAs limit how many homes in the community can be rented at the same time — often to keep the share of owner-occupied homes high, which also matters to mortgage lenders. Once the cap is reached, new owners join a waiting list, and that wait can be months or years. Some communities ban rentals entirely for the first year or two after purchase. None of this is in the listing; it is in the CC&Rs, the declaration every buyer receives and almost nobody reads in full.

## Approval, leases and minimum terms

Even where renting is allowed, the board usually has a say. Common requirements include written approval before a tenant moves in, a minimum lease term of six or twelve months, a ban on short-term or holiday rentals, and a copy of the lease on file with the association. The tenant has to follow the same rules as an owner — parking, noise, pets, bins — and when the tenant breaks a rule, the fine lands on the owner, not the tenant.

## The owner still pays the fees

Renting out the home does not pause the HOA fees. The owner keeps paying the monthly assessment, any special assessment, and any fines the tenant's behaviour causes. Some associations also charge a move-in fee, a tenant registration fee, or a higher deposit for rented homes. A rental that looks profitable on the rent alone can look very different once the fees, the vacancy months and the fines are counted.

## What to check before you buy to rent

Read the rental section of the CC&Rs before you make an offer, not after. Ask the association how many rentals are currently active, whether there is a waiting list, and how long it is. Ask whether short-term rentals are banned, and whether the board can change the rental rules later. Then run the real numbers: rent minus the mortgage, minus the HOA fee, minus a realistic allowance for empty months and repairs. If the maths only works when the home is rented every single month, it does not work.
