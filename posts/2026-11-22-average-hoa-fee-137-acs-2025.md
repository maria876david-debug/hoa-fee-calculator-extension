# The Average HOA Fee in America Is Now $137 a Month

The latest American Community Survey figures (ACS 2025) put the median monthly HOA fee in the United States at $137 — up from $135 in the previous vintage. Two dollars sounds like nothing, but the national median hides a spread so wide that the average is almost the least useful number in the whole table. What matters is where your state and your community sit inside it.

## The spread behind the median

At the top, New York's median is $809 a month — driven by New York City co-ops and condos where the fee covers staffing, elevators and building systems. Florida sits at $234, reflecting condo-heavy housing stock and insurance costs that keep climbing. At the other end, Texas comes in at $81 a month, where most associations cover neighbourhood amenities and common areas rather than whole buildings. Same country, same label on the bill, completely different product.

## Why the median keeps creeping up

Three pressures push fees in one direction. Insurance premiums for shared buildings have risen sharply, and every owner shares the bill. Reserve funds that were underfunded for years are being topped up — sometimes under new state laws requiring proper studies and funding plans. And the everyday contracts — landscaping, pools, security, pest control — renew higher each year. A board that raises fees while showing residents the insurance bill and the reserve study is managing the money; a board that cannot explain an increase is the warning sign.

## What $137 a month really means over time

A monthly fee is never just a monthly fee. At $137, a buyer is committing about $1,644 a year — and over a ten-year stay, with typical annual increases, the total paid in fees alone can pass $18,000. That is money that does not build equity, which is why the fee belongs in the same calculation as the mortgage payment when you compare two homes. A cheaper house with a $400 fee can cost more each month than a pricier one with a $120 fee.

## Before you buy, ask three questions

What does the fee actually cover — building systems, or just the pool and the gates? When was the last reserve study, and is the fund on track? And has the community levied a special assessment in the last five years? The answers tell you more about your future costs than the headline fee.
